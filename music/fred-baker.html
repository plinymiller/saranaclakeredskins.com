Music / Tribute

Saranac Lake band director, 1972–1997[1]

Fred Baker

For 25 years Fred Carl Baker led the instrumental bands of the Saranac Lake Central School District. He was also a saxophonist and the founder of a North Country swing and jazz band.[1][2]

Share a memory or photo

“He influenced students, not only in their music, but in their whole lives.”Adirondack Daily Enterprise obituary, Nov. 25, 2002[1]

His life in music

  1. 1939

    Born July 28 in Granville, son of Edward Baker and Rose Viau Baker.[1]

  2. 1957–60

    Served in the United States Army Band.[1]

  3. Crane

    Studied music at the Crane School of Music in Potsdam and graduated with a degree in music education. He also studied at the Eastman School of Music.[1]

  4. 1965

    Married Karen DeHond in Rochester. While in Rochester he taught music in the Wheatland-Chili and Fairport Central school districts and played in the Chuck Mangione Band.[1]

  5. 1972

    Moved to Saranac Lake with Karen and their daughter Kristina and became the instrumental band director for the Saranac Lake Central School District.[1][2]

  6. 1980

    Founded the 52nd Street Band, which was still playing swing and jazz across the North Country more than two decades later.[1]

  7. Community

    Led the North Country Community Band, founded at North Country Community College in 1969, after its first director, George Reynolds.[4]

  8. 1992

    Was scheduled to direct the Saranac Lake High School stage band at a town hall concert during the village centennial celebration. Helen Demong directed the choir.[5]

  9. 1997

    Retired after 25 years with the school district. He kept playing with small groups and the Wally Siebel Big Band.[1]

  10. Railroad

    Took great joy in serving as a volunteer conductor on the Adirondack Scenic Railroad. He lived in the Carey Cottage beside the tracks near the Union Depot.[1][3]

  11. 2002

    Died suddenly of heart failure on Nov. 22 at St. Peter’s Hospital in Albany. His memorial service was held at the Union Depot in Saranac Lake.[1]
